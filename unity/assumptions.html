Assumptions and parameters

The simulator is an assumption engine, not a forecast. This page shows how each result is produced, which inputs come from published evidence, and which depend on judgement.

The accounting identity

Annual net cost combines the inherited fiscal position, negotiated liabilities, policy harmonisation and transition spending, then subtracts structural savings and revenue from relative growth.

Net costt = core deficitt + pensionst + debt interest + reratingt + investmentt + duplication − structural savingst − growth revenuet

The starting point

The model reconciles the published Northern fiscal deficit to an inherited core deficit.

€16.58bn − €1.59bn defence − €0.67bn other UK-wide spending − €1.79bn tax adjustment − €6.67bn pensions − €4.12bn debt interest = €1.74bn

Rerating

Rerating means raising Northern welfare and public-sector pay towards Southern levels.

€11bn × selected scope × min(year ÷ phase-in years, 1)

A 25 per cent scope phased over 15 years ultimately adds €2.75 billion annually.

Relative growth

Only Northern growth above Southern growth produces convergence revenue.

Relative outputt = ((1 + gN) ÷ (1 + gS))t

The gain is multiplied by Northern output and the 35 per cent revenue share.

Pension transition

If the UK retains legacy rights, new Irish pension responsibility accumulates gradually.

min(year × €0.115bn, €6.67bn) × ageing factor

This is a provisional interpretation of the study’s transition description.

What break-even means

  • Annual break-even is the first year in which that year’s simulated fiscal balance reaches zero
  • Cumulative payback is the first year in which later surpluses offset every earlier deficit
  • Neither measure implies full productivity or income convergence

What the model omits

  • Population, migration and pension cohorts
  • Marginal public-service costs generated by higher output
  • Behavioural tax responses and general-equilibrium effects
  • Independent returns from transition investment
  • Detailed costs of public-service quality convergence

Interpretation warning

The optimistic glide-path result is driven materially by 5 per cent Northern growth against 2.5 per cent Southern growth. The resulting 2.44 per cent relative-growth differential is an upside assumption, not an established forecast.
