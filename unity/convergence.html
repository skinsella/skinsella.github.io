A decision framework

Three convergence tests

Unity is economically credible only if the transition is affordable, Northern convergence generates its own revenue, and public services can improve without destabilising either jurisdiction.

Core modelAdvanced modelEvidenceConvergence testsAssumptions

Test 1

Fiscal resilience

Can the transition be financed without an excessive peak cost, an unstable debt path, or permanent reliance on additional taxation?

The 2 per cent and 10 per cent limits are proposed decision thresholds, not statutory fiscal rules.

Test 2

Productive convergence

Does convergence create enough employment and revenue to cover recurring harmonisation costs rather than merely shifting transfers?

A model pass remains provisional until observed productivity, employment, earnings and revenue data confirm the path.

Test 3

Services and institutions

Can health, education, housing, welfare and administrative systems converge without a deterioration in outcomes?

Data gap

The current model estimates fiscal and cohort pressures but does not yet contain comparable outcome series. This test cannot honestly pass or fail until those data are assembled.

Combined decision rule

All three tests should pass under the central path, remain broadly viable under the cautious path, and avoid catastrophic failure under the adverse settlement. A result that depends on one optimistic growth rate or one favourable negotiation outcome is provisional rather than robust.
